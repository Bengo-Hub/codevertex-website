# Plum Grid — Design Philosophy

**Plum Grid** is Swiss formalism given a warm, African-rooted voice. Its foundation is the
modular grid: a strict column system where every line of type, every photograph and every
rule snaps to the same invisible scaffolding. Order is not decoration here; it is the message.
A structure this disciplined says, without words, that the systems behind it are engineered.

Colour is limited and deliberate. A single deep plum — drawn directly from the vertex mark —
carries all emphasis, set against warm paper white and near-black ink. Gold and a dark, burnt orange are the
second voice: angled colour strips, rules and small markers that appear only where the eye must
pause, giving the plum warmth and momentum. Nothing is tinted for its own sake; each
chromatic decision is meticulously calibrated so that the palette reads as one voice from five
metres away and as a finely tuned instrument from arm's length.

Typography is neo-grotesque — Helvetica and its descendants — used at two extremes: monumental
display weights that anchor the composition, and small, tightly tracked labels that behave like
annotations on an engineering drawing. Indexed numerals (01, 02, 03…) turn lists into
specifications. The contrast in scale does the work that paragraphs cannot.

Photography is treated as evidence, not ornament: real workspaces, real screens, cropped hard
to the grid and grounded with a plum veil so they belong to the same material as the type.
Images and text never compete; they take turns, and the rhythm between them is the product of
painstaking spatial orchestration.

Every margin, baseline and gutter should look labored over by a master typographer — nothing
crowding, nothing floating, nothing accidental. The finished piece should feel like the work of
countless careful hours: calm, exact, and confident enough to leave space empty.
